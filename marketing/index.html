Human-in-the-loop intrusion detection

Machine-speed detection.
Human judgement.

A live network monitoring dashboard. Machine learning flags suspicious traffic within seconds, and your analysts decide what is real. Every decision they make helps the model get better.

Why we are building this

Too many alerts

Security teams get buried in alerts. When most of them are noise, the real attacks are easy to miss.

Black-box models

Many ML detectors give a verdict with no context, so analysts cannot tell how much to trust it.

Models go stale

Networks change over time. A model trained once slowly drifts and starts raising the wrong alerts.

How it works

Four steps, running as one continuous loop.

  1. 1

    Capture

    A small sensor on your network watches live traffic with CICFlowMeter and turns each connection into a flow record. It sends a heartbeat every 30 seconds and keeps flows safe if the server is briefly unreachable.

  2. 2

    Detect

    Two models score every flow. A Random Forest spots known attack types, and an Isolation Forest flags unusual behaviour it has never seen before. Each alert comes with a confidence score and a severity.

  3. 3

    Review

    Alerts stream straight onto the dashboard. Analysts investigate and close each one as a True Positive, Benign Positive or False Positive.

  4. 4

    Learn

    Those decisions become training labels. Retraining uses them and shows before-and-after scores, so the model gets better at telling real threats from noise in your network.

What you get

Live alert feed

New alerts appear on the dashboard as they happen, pushed over WebSocket. No refreshing.

Known attacks and anomalies

Catch familiar attack categories, plus strange behaviour that does not match anything seen before.

Confidence and severity

Every alert shows how sure the model is and how serious the threat could be, from low to critical.

Three-way decisions

Benign Positive separates "real but harmless" from "wrong", giving the model clearer feedback.

Retrain on demand

Retrain the model on your team's labels and compare its scores before and after.

Reliable sensor

Heartbeats show the sensor is alive, and a local buffer resends flows after a network drop.
